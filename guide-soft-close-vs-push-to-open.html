5 min read

Soft close
or push to open?

They solve different problems and they interact, which is why a handleless kitchen has to decide between them or pay for the mechanism that does both.

The short answer

Soft close slows a door or drawer over the last few centimetres so it cannot slam. Push to open releases a catch when you press the front, so no handle is needed. The complication is that they work against each other: a push-to-open catch needs to spring the front outward, and a soft-close damper is resisting movement at exactly that point. Combining them needs a mechanism built for it, which exists and costs more. Blum and the other quality brands make it; the cheap end does not.

What each is for

Soft close is about longevity and noise. A drawer slammed daily for ten years loosens its joints and its runners; a damper removes that entirely, and it is why we fit it as standard. Push to open is about appearance — it lets a kitchen have no handles at all, without a shadowline or a J-pull.

Why they conflict

Push to open works by holding the front under slight tension and releasing it when pressed. Soft close resists movement at the end of travel. Put a basic version of each on the same drawer and the drawer either will not spring open or will not close softly. The combined mechanisms are engineered to sequence the two, which is why they are a separate, dearer product line.

Which to choose

If you want handles or a shadowline, take soft close everywhere and be done. If you want a completely handle-free kitchen, decide whether the extra cost of combined mechanisms is worth it, or accept push to open without damping on those fronts. Ask us directly →
Can you have soft close and push to open together?

Yes, but it needs a mechanism designed for it, which costs more. Basic versions of each fight one another on the same drawer.

Is push to open reliable?

Quality mechanisms are. The failures people describe usually come from cheap catches that lose tension, or from combining them with damping that was not designed to work together.

Do I need push to open for a handleless kitchen?

No. A shadowline or a handle rail gives the same handle-free look while keeping ordinary soft-close hardware.

Is soft close standard?

On our cabinetry, yes, on every hinge and runner. It is not standard across the industry, which is worth asking any supplier directly.
