Choosing
a benchtop.

The most-touched surface in the house, and the one where a saving costs you most.

Design your kitchen See pricing

Quartz and granite, briefly

Quartz (engineered stone) is crushed stone bound in resin. It is non-porous, so it does not need sealing and will not stain from wine or oil left overnight. Colour and pattern are consistent, because they are manufactured, which makes it predictable when you are choosing from a sample.

Granite is quarried, so every slab is different and the one you get is genuinely unique. It is extremely hard and handles heat better than engineered stone. Being natural it is slightly porous and wants periodic sealing.

Choosing between them is mostly about whether you want consistency or individuality. Both are standard here, so it is not a budget decision.

Marble is available as a paid upgrade. It is the most beautiful and the least forgiving — softer, and it etches where acid touches it. Lemon juice leaves a mark. People who love marble know this and accept it as patina; people who do not should not buy it.

Why there is no laminate or timber option

This is a deliberate restriction and it is worth explaining rather than presenting as a feature.

Laminate is a perfectly reasonable surface and it is genuinely cheaper. Its weakness is the edge: wherever water reaches the substrate — around a sink cut-out, at a join, behind a tap — the board swells and the laminate lifts, and once it has, it cannot be repaired.

Timber is beautiful and it moves. It needs oiling, it marks, and it responds to humidity. In much of Queensland humidity is a constant rather than a season.

Offering either would make the headline price lower and the kitchen shorter-lived, in the specific climate most of our customers are in. If a laminate benchtop is what your budget needs, that is a legitimate choice — it is just not one we supply.

The practical things nobody mentions

Nothing is heatproof. Put a hot pan on a trivet regardless of material. Engineered stone in particular can suffer thermal shock.

Sink cut-outs are decided before manufacture. Buy the sink first and use its actual cut-out dimensions.

Stone is heavy. Roughly 45–50 kg per square metre at 20 mm, which matters if you are building a tiny home on wheels and matters not at all on a slab.

Questions

Benchtops, specifically.

Quartz or granite — which is better?

Neither, generally. Quartz is non-porous and consistent, so it needs no sealing and looks like the sample. Granite is unique to the slab and handles heat better but wants periodic sealing. Both are standard, so it is a preference rather than a budget decision.

Why do you not offer laminate?

Because of the edge. Wherever water reaches the substrate — around a sink cut-out, at a join, behind a tap — the board swells and the laminate lifts, and it cannot be repaired. In a humid climate that happens sooner. Laminate is a legitimate choice; it is just not one we supply.

Can I have a timber benchtop?

No. Timber moves with humidity, and much of Queensland is humid for most of the year. It needs oiling and it marks.

Is marble a good idea?

It is the most beautiful and the least forgiving — softer than granite, and it etches where acid touches it. Lemon juice leaves a mark. It is available as a paid upgrade for people who want it knowing that.

How much does a stone benchtop add?

Nothing extra — a quartz or granite benchtop is included in every range price. Marble is the only paid upgrade.
