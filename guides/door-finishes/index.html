The doors are 90% of what you see and most of what you touch. This is where the money shows.

The four families, and what separates them

Matching melamine — the same board as the carcass, edged. The most economical option, entirely serviceable, and the right answer for a rental, a shed kitchen or anywhere the budget has to go elsewhere.

Wrapped MDF (thermolaminated) — a vinyl film heat-wrapped over a routed MDF door. This is where profiles become possible: shaker, V-groove, a soft matt surface. The most common choice in a family kitchen, and the best balance of look and cost.

High-gloss — a mirror finish that bounces light around a room and makes a small kitchen feel larger. It also shows every fingerprint, which in a house with small children is a daily fact rather than a footnote.

2Pac painted — sprayed and cured, giving the deepest, most even colour and the closest thing to bespoke joinery. The longest lead time and the highest cost.

Choosing for the room rather than the photograph

Every finish looks good in a showroom photograph, taken once, in controlled light, before anyone cooked in it. Choose for how the room is actually used.

Rental or investment: melamine or wrapped MDF. Hard-wearing, cheap to replace a damaged door, and nobody is polishing it between tenants.

Family kitchen: wrapped MDF in a matt finish. Forgiving of fingerprints and handprints, and the profile options make it look considered rather than plain.

Small or dark room: gloss genuinely helps, if you can live with wiping it.

The kitchen you intend to keep: 2Pac, if the lead time suits.

Lead time is part of the decision

Finishes are not equally quick. The wrapped, gloss and painted options each carry their own manufacturing lead time, and 2Pac is the longest of them.

On a renovation with a family living in the house, or a rental between tenancies, that difference has a cost that does not appear on the quote. The planner shows the lead time against each finish so it is a visible part of the choice rather than a surprise after ordering.

Questions

Finishes, specifically.

Which finish is most durable?

For hard use, melamine and wrapped MDF are the most forgiving and the cheapest to replace a single damaged door. 2Pac gives the best finish but a repair is a respray rather than a swap.

What is thermolaminated or wrapped MDF?

A vinyl film heat-wrapped over a routed MDF door. It is what makes profiles like shaker and V-groove possible, and it is the most common choice in a family kitchen.

Is high-gloss hard to keep clean?

It wipes clean easily but shows fingerprints readily, which is a daily reality in a house with young children. In a small or dark room the light it bounces around is often worth it.

Which finish should I use in a rental?

Melamine or wrapped MDF. Hard-wearing, inexpensive to replace a single door, and not dependent on anyone maintaining them.

Do different finishes have different lead times?

Yes, and it is worth factoring in. The wrapped, gloss and painted options each carry their own manufacturing lead time, with 2Pac the longest. The planner shows the lead time against each finish.
