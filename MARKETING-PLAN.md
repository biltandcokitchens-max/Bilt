# Bilt & Co — marketing plan

Written 30 August 2026. Designed around one constraint: **one hour a week, every week.**

Anything that needs more than that has been deliberately left out, because a plan you abandon in
week three is worth less than no plan at all.

---

## Where the business actually stands

| | |
|---|---|
| Kitchens sold | ~75, in about a year |
| Where they came from | **100% word of mouth** |
| Marketing channels | None |
| Photographs of own work | None |
| Google reviews | None |
| Social media | None |
| Constraint | **Not enough enquiries** — there is spare capacity |

Two things follow from that. The product is good — 75 referral sales means people actively
recommend you. And growth has stalled because a social graph only reaches so far.

There is also a leak. Someone is told *"use Bilt & Co"*, googles the name, and finds no Google
profile, no reviews and no photographs. Referrals are being lost at the search step, and no amount
of posting fixes that. Reviews do.

---

## The structure: stock is the hook, designer is the business

You have three things to sell, and they do different jobs.

| | What it is | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **The display kitchen** | Installed in the tiny home, can be touched | Getting people in front of the product |
| **Two boxed kitchens** | Stock, available now, no lead time | Creating enquiries with no friction |
| **Designer kitchens** | $15,000 – $47,000, 8–12 weeks | The actual business |

### The one thing to get right

**Do not lead with the price.**

If the ad says "$6,500 kitchen", everyone who responds is anchored at $6,500, and moving someone
from there to $30,000 is very hard. Lead with the display instead — *"a kitchen you can actually
open the drawers on"*. The boxed stock will sell to whoever wants it without being shouted about.
The display is what generates designer leads.

To be precise, because these two things can look contradictory: **the Marketplace listings for the
boxed kitchens carry a price** — they are product listings and they have to. What should never lead
with a price is everything else: the page, the posts, the conversation, the display. Sell the two
boxed kitchens as products; sell everything else on the fact that people can come and see one.

The display also solves a real problem: you have no premises, so until now there was nowhere for a
customer to see the product before committing several thousand dollars. Now there is.

## The weekly hour

| Minutes | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **30** | Trade outreach | Two or three builders. The main engine. |
| **15** | One social post | One photo, two lines, to Facebook and Google Business Profile. Early on you only have the display kitchen to show, so this will be occasional rather than weekly until the portfolio starts building around month three. Do not manufacture things to post. |
| **15** | Capture admin | Filing photos, answering enquiries, chasing reviews. |

That is the whole commitment. If a week gets away from you, the 30 minutes of trade outreach is the
part to protect — it is the only one that produces enquiries directly.

---

## The capture habit

Every job, no exceptions, before you leave site:

1. **Ten photographs on your phone.** Wide shot, two or three angles, one detail of the hardware,
   one of the benchtop edge. Five minutes. Natural light, blinds open, bench cleared.
2. **Ask for the review, in person, standing in the finished kitchen.** *"If you're happy with how
   it turned out, a Google review would genuinely help us — most people find us by word of mouth
   and it's the first thing they check."*

That second point matters. Ringing a customer months later is awkward, which is why it does not get
done. Asking at handover, while they are delighted and looking at the result, is a completely
different act and almost nobody says no. It is not a workaround — it is the version that works.

In six months this alone produces a real portfolio and a real review count, from work you were
doing anyway.

## Trade outreach — the engine

Builders do not read Google reviews the way homeowners do. They care about price, reliability and
lead time, which means **you can win trade work before you have any social proof at all.** That is
why this is the engine and not the afterthought.

**Who to approach, in priority order:**

1. Tiny home builders — they buy repeatedly, to a repeating spec
2. Granny flat and secondary dwelling specialists — same
3. Project builders doing new homes in Rockhampton and Gracemere
4. Carpenters and renovation builders who currently subcontract cabinetry

**What to say.** Not a pitch — an invitation:

> "I supply kitchens out of Rockhampton — designed, delivered assembled and installed, or supply
> only if you'd rather fit them yourself. I've got a display kitchen set up you're welcome to come
> and look at. If you tell me what you build I'll put trade pricing in front of you."

Three a week is 150 a year. You do not need 150 — you need four good ones.

**Before you start**, be ready to answer the three questions every builder asks, because the
`trade.html` page currently says terms are quoted rather than published:

- What is the trade discount?
- What is the minimum order?
- What are the payment terms?

## Social media — built slowly, in this order

The point is not to become a content creator. It is to exist, look real, and be findable. Each step
below is a one-off setup, not an ongoing commitment.

### Step 1 — Google Business Profile *(in progress)*

Not social media, but more important than all of it combined. Roughly a third of local ranking
weight. Finish the video verification, then complete every field. Setup copy is in the session
notes; categories, services and description are already written.

### Step 2 — Facebook page *(month 1, ~30 minutes)*

You need this regardless of whether you enjoy it:

- It is where Marketplace listings live
- It is what people check when they are deciding whether you are real
- It links to the website and the Google profile as a trust signal

Set it up with: the display kitchen photos, `biltstudio.com.au`, the mobile number, Rockhampton as
the service area, and the same description used on the Google profile. Then list the **two boxed
kitchens on Marketplace**.

**Where to post, beyond your own page:**

- Rockhampton buy/swap/sell and community groups
- Central Queensland trades and building groups
- **Tiny home groups — these are national and very active.** You have a tiny home with a kitchen in
  it, which is native content there rather than an advertisement, and tiny-home builders are
  precisely the trade buyer you want

### Step 3 — Instagram *(month 2, ~20 minutes)*

Create it linked to the Facebook page through Meta Business Suite so **one post publishes to both**.
That is the entire reason to do it — it costs no additional weekly time. Kitchens are visual and
people expect a business like yours to have one.

### Step 4 — Pinterest *(month 6, once there is a portfolio)*

Worth knowing about because it is misunderstood: Pinterest is a **search engine, not a feed**. A pin
of a good kitchen keeps sending traffic for years, and kitchen content performs there better than
almost any other category. It needs real photographs, so it waits until you have them.

### Not doing

TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, X, a blog schedule, or an agency. Each needs more time than exists or
more assets than you have.

### One follow-up job for the website

Once the Facebook, Instagram and Google profiles exist, their URLs should be added to the site's
business schema as `sameAs`. That is how Google connects the website to the business entity, and it
is one of the cheapest ranking wins available. Ask and it takes ten minutes.

## Paid advertising — later, not now

Ads work far better with photographs and reviews behind them. Running them now would mean paying to
send strangers to a page with stock imagery and no social proof.

Revisit once there are **ten reviews and twenty photographs of your own work** — realistically
around month four to six. Then Google Search ads pointed at `investment.html` are the first thing to
test, because that page answers the highest-intent question in the category.

## Measuring it

Four numbers, one line in a notebook each week:

```
Week __   Enquiries: __   From: __________   Reviews: __   Jobs photographed: __
```

Recording **where each enquiry came from** is the important one. Within two months it tells you
which half of the hour is working, and you can stop doing the other half.

**If enquiries have not moved by week eight**, change the trade outreach before anything else —
either who you are approaching or what you are offering them.

## The first ninety days

| | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–2** | Finish Google Business Profile. Set up the Facebook page. Photograph the display kitchen properly — this is your entire visual library right now, so give it a full afternoon. |
| **Weeks 3–4** | List the two boxed kitchens on Marketplace. Begin trade outreach at three a week. Settle the three trade questions above. |
| **Weeks 5–8** | Trade outreach continues. Capture habit on every job. First reviews should appear. Instagram linked to Facebook. |
| **Weeks 9–12** | First real portfolio images from completed jobs. Start posting weekly rather than sporadically. Review what the enquiry log is telling you and cut whatever is not working. |

---

## What this plan is honest about

- **It is slow at the start**, because the assets are being built from zero. The trade engine is
  what carries the first few months.
- **It depends on the capture habit.** Skip the photos and reviews at handover and in six months you
  will be exactly where you are now.
- **Word of mouth remains your best channel.** None of this replaces it — the reviews and photos
  exist mostly to stop referrals falling through the crack between hearing your name and finding
  nothing when they search it.
