Benchtop thickness,
and what it really means.

A thicker benchtop is mostly a visual decision, and the thick edge you are looking at is usually not solid material all the way through.

The short answer

Stone and porcelain benchtops are commonly supplied at 20mm and finished with either a 20mm edge or a mitred edge built up to look 40mm thick. A built-up edge uses a strip of the same material mitred at the corner, so the top looks solid without doubling the weight or the cost. Thickness changes the look and the price; it rarely changes durability, because the substrate and the support underneath do that work.

What thickness actually affects

Appearance first. A 20mm top reads light and contemporary; a 40mm edge reads substantial and traditional. Weight second @EM@ a genuinely solid 40mm slab is heavy enough to affect what the cabinets and the floor have to carry. Cost third, and mostly through the extra fabrication a built-up edge needs rather than the material itself.

Mitred versus solid

A mitred edge joins a strip of the same slab at 45 degrees so the pattern runs around the corner and the join is almost invisible when done well. It is the normal way to get a thick look. A genuinely solid 40mm slab exists but costs considerably more and weighs roughly twice as much. Our guide to mitred joins covers how that edge is made.

Laminate is different

Laminate benchtops come in standard thicknesses from the manufacturer, commonly around 33mm, with a postformed or square edge. You are choosing from what is made rather than specifying a thickness, and the decision is really about edge profile and colour.

Ask us directly →
Is a 40mm benchtop better than 20mm?

Not structurally. It is a visual choice. Durability comes from the material and the support under it, not from the thickness of the edge.

What is a mitred edge?

A strip of the same slab joined at 45 degrees to make a 20mm top look 40mm thick, with the pattern running around the corner.

Does a thicker benchtop cost more?

Usually, mostly through the extra fabrication rather than the material. It is quoted as its own line so you can see the difference.

How thick are laminate benchtops?

Laminate comes in manufacturer standard thicknesses, commonly around 33mm. You choose the edge profile rather than the thickness.
