What actually changes

Nobody misses their old kitchen.
They miss the hour it used to take.

The difference a kitchen makes is not visible in a photograph. It is in four or five moments a day that either work or irritate, every day, for about fifteen years.

Six in the morning

The light comes on as the drawer opens, so you are not squinting at a dark cupboard. The coffee, the cups and the bin are within one turn of each other because somebody drew them that way. You are out of the house eight minutes earlier and you never work out why.

Unpacking the shopping

Everything has somewhere, and the somewhere is where you would look for it. The pantry comes out to you instead of you going into it. Nothing lives at the back, so nothing goes off at the back.

Two people, one room

The dishwasher is open and you can still get to the sink. Somebody is at the island not in the way. This is almost entirely a question of clearances, and it is the single thing people get wrong when they draw their own.

When people come over

The bench is clear because the things that usually cover it have a home. You are in the room with everyone instead of facing a wall. The kitchen stops being the place you apologise for.

Why it still feels like that in ten years

The boring part
is the whole point.

None of the specification below is interesting. It is worth knowing because it is the difference between a kitchen that still feels like the first morning and one that starts to annoy you in the third year, and because almost nobody quoting you will put it in writing.

We print it on the quote so you can hold it against anything else you are offered — the rates are published too.

  • 18mm moisture-resistant board. The panel under the sink is still flat in ten years. You will never think about it, which is the point.
  • Laser-bonded edging, no glue line. Nothing for water to track along, so the edge by the dishwasher does not lift and go dark.
  • Soft-close hinges, full-extension runners. Nobody wakes the baby closing a cupboard, and the pan at the back comes to you instead of you kneeling on the floor.
  • Cut to your wall, not to catalogue widths. No filler panel. The run goes wall to wall and looks like it was always there.
  • Hardware brand named on your quote. Not described, named — so you can compare it with the quote that does not.
